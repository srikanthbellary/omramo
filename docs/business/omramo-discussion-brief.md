# Omramo discussion brief for Fable 5.2 design mockups (Srikanth, 13 Nov 2026)

Use this brief together with business case v5 (`2026-11-13-omramo-business-use-case-v5-story-tiers.md`) and the public-domain story PDFs in `stories/`. Every design mockup must follow all 15 points.

1. Omramo is a global app in English. It is not Telugu-first.
2. It is illustration-driven: reading books with a pseudo-video-game feel, for children who can already read.
3. Stories are the core. There's an existing story list, and new stories will be added.
4. Kids can tap things in a story (for example "Taj Mahal") to see a visualization, and characters appear as visuals.
5. It is oriented around reading books, designed to capture young minds.
6. It is literally a magical book, not video storytelling. It's heavily illustrated, but the child reads the text on the page.
7. The goal is to get kids reading; games and short video are the competition pulling them away. Illustrations and tap-to-see pull kids into the text and never replace it.
8. Stories are leveled Low, Medium and Advanced by complexity, so kids can choose by age.
9. Tiers are locked by the story's message. Each unique story belongs to exactly one tier, and no story appears in more than one. Advanced stories carry deeper meanings; lower tiers carry basic morals.
10. It's for the foldable iPhone Duo, built natively in Swift, with a two-pane layout where the tapped visual opens beside the text.
11. Business model: a family subscription and a free sample, with school licences later. No ads or tracking, and a parental gate.
12. Parents see pages read, not screen time.
13. Rewards come only from reading, such as a bookshelf and reading streaks.
14. Three prototype books, one per tier.
15. Competition: Epic and Raz-Kids. Tap-to-see and the fold are what Omramo has to prove.
16. The stories are educational: they teach children about countries and cultures, especially major historical civilizations. The catalog draws on stories from significant civilizations and cultures, such as India (Panchatantra or Jataka tales), China, Japan, African cultures (for example Anansi tales from the Akan/Ashanti tradition), and other historically significant cultures. That way children learn about the world's great cultures by reading. The three public-domain prototype stories should each come from a different major civilization or culture where possible, still one per tier by depth of message. Record each story's civilization or country of origin with its source, and the mockups can show it, for example as a map pin or a culture note on the cover or a tap-to-see card.
