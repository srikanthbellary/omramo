## Why each tier
- **Low — basic moral:** keep your wits; a clever answer beats a bigger foe. Short sentences, two short parts.
- **Medium — a bigger idea:** no one can own all the wisdom; even a child can teach the wise, and wisdom belongs to everyone.
- **Advanced — deeper meaning:** kindness rewarded, homesickness, time and loss, a broken promise that cannot be undone; long text, richer vocabulary.

## Public-domain basis
- All three editions were published before 1931, so they are in the public domain in the United States (works published before 1 Jan 1931 entered the US public domain by 2026). Project Gutenberg distributes each as US public domain.
- Author deaths: Ozaki died 1932 (94 years ago). Babbitt, Barker and Sinclair: death years not verified here; US status rests on publication date. Outside the US, check local term (life + 70 in many countries) before shipping these exact retellings.
- The underlying folktales (Jataka, Akan Anansi tradition, Urashima legend) are traditional, centuries old.
- **Illustrations:** none are included in these PDFs. The original book illustrations (Young 1912, Sinclair 1917, Fujiyama 1903/1908) are also pre-1931 and therefore US public domain, but Omramo mockups use original placeholder art only.
- Note: the Babbitt and Barker retellings were written for colonial-era / early-1900s audiences; review wording for cultural accuracy and respect before production (e.g., Barker's book includes colonial-era commentary outside the story text).

## How the PDFs were made
Project Gutenberg plain-text (UTF-8) downloaded 13 Nov 2026 to `src/`; the single story was extracted, wrapped in HTML with a source header, and printed to PDF with headless Google Chrome on the box. Gutenberg boilerplate removed from the story PDFs; full original files kept in `src/`.
