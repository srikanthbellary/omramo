# Part I

# --- page 1
This very old fable from Greece begins high in the sky.

The North Wind was boasting again. He was a great grey cloud with puffed-out cheeks.

“I am the strongest thing in the sky!” he boomed. “Stronger than rain. Stronger than thunder. Stronger than you!”

The Sun smiled, and said nothing at all.

# --- page 2
Far below lay the blue Aegean Sea, dotted with islands. A pale road ran along the hills above it.

Along that road came a traveller, wrapped in a thick woollen cloak.

“Do you see that man?” said the Sun. “Whoever can get his cloak off him is the stronger. Shall we try?”

# --- page 3
“Easy!” laughed the North Wind. “I will have it off before he can blink. Me first!”

“You first,” said the Sun, and slipped behind a cloud to watch.

The North Wind swooped down to the hill road. He took a little breath, and he puffed.

# --- page 4
The olive trees shivered. Their leaves flashed silver, and dust skipped along the road.

“Brr,” said the traveller. “The wind is getting up.”

He tucked in his chin, wrapped himself up snugly, and held his cloak tighter.

“Hmph,” said the North Wind. “That was only a puff.”

# --- page 5
So he took a bigger breath, and he blew.

Off flew the traveller’s hat. The goats’ bells clanged on the hillside, and far below the waves turned white.

“My hat!” cried the traveller. He ran, he caught it, he jammed it back on, and held his cloak tighter.

# --- page 6
Now the North Wind was cross. He took the biggest breath of his life, and he HOWLED.

Branches cracked. Fishing boats ran for the shore. Sand stung the traveller’s knees.

He bent almost double. He gripped the wool in both fists, and held his cloak tighter than ever.

# --- page 7
The North Wind puffed. He blew. He wheezed. Then he had no breath left at all.

He flopped onto a hilltop like a wet grey blanket.

“It cannot be done,” he panted. “That cloak is stuck to him. Nobody could get it off.”

“May I try?” asked the Sun.

# Part II

# --- page 8
The Sun came out from behind the cloud, quiet as a cat.

No puffing. No blowing. Only a soft gold light on the road.

The stones grew warm. The bees came back to the thyme. The sea lay down smooth and blue.

“That is better,” said the traveller, and he let his cloak hang loose.

# --- page 9
The Sun shone a little warmer.

Lizards crept out to bask on the wall. The air smelled of hot pine.

“Phew,” said the traveller. He pushed back his hat. Then he undid the pin at his shoulder.

On his hilltop, the North Wind opened one eye.

# --- page 10
The Sun shone warmer still.

“Too hot for wool!” said the traveller, and he laughed out loud.

Off came the cloak. He shook it, folded it, and tucked it under his arm.

“Oh,” said the North Wind, in a very small voice.

# --- page 11
The traveller cooled his dusty feet in the sea at a little harbour. Then he climbed to an old olive tree and lay in its shade, the folded cloak beside him.

“That is not fair,” grumbled the North Wind. “I worked so hard. You hardly did a thing!”

“You pushed, my friend,” said the Sun. “I only asked.”

# --- page 12
The afternoon grew hot and still. Under the olive tree, the traveller fanned himself with his hat.

Then a small, cool breeze came up from the sea. It did not puff or blow or howl. It only lifted his hair.

“Ah,” said the traveller. “Thank you, wind.”

And high over the islands, the North Wind went quite pink with pleasure.
