# Part I

# --- page 1
An old Donkey had carried sacks to the mill for many years.

Now his legs were slow and his back was sore.

One morning he heard his master grumble at the gate.

“That Donkey is too old to be useful,” the man said. “He is not worth his oats.”

# --- page 2
The Donkey’s long ears drooped. Then up they went again.

“Too old?” he said. “My legs are slow, but my voice is as loud as ever. Hee-haw!”

“I know! I will go to Bremen, the great town by the river. They love music there.”

So he pushed open the gate and set off down the road.

# --- page 3
Soon he met an old Dog lying by the road, puffing and panting.

“Why are you puffing so, friend?” asked the Donkey.

“I cannot run fast any more,” puffed the Dog. “My master says I am no use. Where shall I go?”

“Come with me to Bremen. I am going to be a town musician. You have a fine voice!”

# --- page 4
The Dog wagged his tail, and on they went, two together.

Before long they met an old Cat in the road, looking as gloomy as a week of rain.

“My teeth are blunt,” she sighed. “I would rather sit by the fire than chase mice. So nobody wants me.”

“Come with us to Bremen. You have a fine voice!”

# --- page 5
The Cat licked a paw, and on they went, three together.

At the next farm a Rooster stood on the gate, crowing with all his might.

“Guests are coming on Sunday,” he cried, “and the cook wants me for the soup pot! So I am crowing while I can!”

“Come with us to Bremen instead. You have a fine voice!”

# --- page 6
Down flew the Rooster, and on they went, four together.

But Bremen was far, too far for one day. When the sky turned pale, they stopped at the edge of a forest.

The Donkey and the Dog lay down under a big tree. The Cat climbed onto a branch. The Rooster flew to the very top.

# --- page 7
From up there he saw something twinkle, far off among the trees.

“A light!” he crowed. “A little yellow light! There must be a house.”

“A house may have a bone in it,” said the Dog.

“And a warm fire,” said the Cat. So up they all got and walked toward the light.

# Part II

# --- page 8
The light shone from a house deep in the forest.

The Donkey was the tallest, so he looked in at the window.

“What do you see, Donkey?” whispered the Rooster.

“A table full of good things,” said the Donkey, “bread and cheese and fat sausages. And three robbers eating it all up.”

# --- page 9
“That supper would suit us,” said the Dog. “But how do we get them out?”

The four friends put their heads together, and soon they had a plan.

The Donkey set his front hooves on the window sill. The Dog climbed onto the Donkey. The Cat climbed onto the Dog. And the Rooster flew up onto the Cat’s head.

# --- page 10
“Now,” whispered the Donkey. “One, two, three, music!”

The Donkey brayed. The Dog barked. The Cat miaowed. The Rooster crowed. Hee-haw! Woof! Miaow! Cock-a-doodle-doo!

Then Crash! In they all tumbled through the window.

The robbers jumped up. They were sure a monster had come for them, and they ran off into the forest as fast as they could go.

# --- page 11
Then the four friends sat down to supper. They ate until every plate shone.

After that they blew out the candle, and each found the bed they liked best.

The Donkey lay on the straw in the yard. The Dog lay behind the door. The Cat curled up by the warm fire. The Rooster flew up to the roof.

# --- page 12
In the dark of night, one robber crept back to look.

Two small lights glowed by the fire. “Hot coals,” he thought, and bent to light his candle.

But they were the Cat’s eyes! She hissed and scratched. The Dog nipped his leg. The Donkey gave him a kick.

And from the roof the Rooster screamed, “Cock-a-doodle-doo!”

# --- page 13
The robber ran all the way back to his friends.

“Do not go near that house!” he puffed. “A witch scratched my face, and a watchman with a stick poked my leg.”

“A great monster thumped me with a club. And up on the roof sat a judge, shouting, ‘Bring that rascal to me!’”

# --- page 14
The robbers never came back.

And the four friends liked the house so well that they stayed.

The Donkey had his straw, the Dog his door, the Cat her fire and the Rooster his roof. Every evening they made music together.

As for Bremen, they never got there. “Bremen can wait,” said the Donkey. And it is waiting still.
