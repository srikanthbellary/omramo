# Part I

# --- page 1
Long ago, the Sun and the Water both lived on the earth.

They were great friends.

The Water’s home was down where the wide river meets the sea. Mangrove trees stood there on long legs, with their toes in the mud.

Every few days the Sun paddled down in his canoe to visit.

# --- page 2
The two friends talked and laughed all afternoon. The Water’s laugh was deep, like waves rolling in.

But the Water never came to the Sun’s house. Not once.

So one day the Sun asked him. “My friend, I visit you so often. Why do you never visit me?”

# --- page 3
“Ah,” said the Water in his deep, slow voice. “Your house is too small, my friend.”

“I never go anywhere alone. My people come with me, and my people are many. We would push you right out of your own door.”

The Sun laughed. But the Water was not joking.

# --- page 4
“If you want me to come,” said the Water, “build a big compound. A very big one. Very big indeed!”

“I will!” said the Sun at once. “I promise. I will build the biggest compound you ever saw.”

He promised it quickly. He did not stop to ask how many people the Water had.

# --- page 5
The Sun hurried home. His wife, the Moon, met him at the door with a wide, bright smile.

“The Water is coming to visit!” he said. “We must build a compound big enough for all his people.”

“How many people?” asked the Moon.

“Many,” said the Sun. “I forgot to ask.”

# --- page 6
“Then we had better build it very big,” said the Moon calmly.

So they began the very next morning. They pressed red earth into thick walls. They swept a yard as wide as a field.

They tied palm leaves into thatch for a great roof.

# --- page 7
Day after day they built. Wall after wall, room after room, roof after roof.

At last it was done. It was bigger than the market. It was the biggest compound anyone had ever seen.

“There,” said the Sun, wiping his hands. “Room for everybody.”

The Moon looked at the great empty yard. “We shall see,” she said.

# Part II

# --- page 8
The next morning, a deep voice called at the gate.

“Is it safe for me to come in?”

“Yes, come in, my friend!” called the Sun.

Whoosh! In flowed the Water, cool and shining, across the yard. With him came little silver fish and crabs that walked sideways.

# --- page 9
More water came, and more. Soon it was as deep as the Sun’s knees.

Fish swam in at the door. A crab sat down on the Moon’s best stool.

“Is it still safe for me to come in?” asked the Water.

“Yes, yes, come in, my friend!” said the Sun. He had promised, after all.

# --- page 10
In came bigger fish, and prawns with long whiskers, and more crabs than anyone could count.

The water rose as high as a tall man’s head.

The Sun and the Moon scrambled up onto the roof.

“My friend,” rumbled the Water, “do you still want my people to come in?”

# --- page 11
The Sun looked at the Moon. The Moon looked at the Sun.

It is hard to send away a guest you begged to come.

“Yes,” said the Sun, more quietly. “Come in, my friend.”

So in came the rest of the Water’s people. Up went the water, over the walls, over the thatch, over the top of the roof.

# --- page 12
There was nowhere left to stand. So the Sun took the Moon’s hand, and up they went.

Up past the palm tops. Up past the birds. Up, up, up into the sky.

Far below, the Water spread out wide and calm. Only the tip of the roof showed.

“Well,” said the Moon, “there is plenty of room up here.”

# --- page 13
And there they have lived ever since. The Sun shines from the sky by day. The Moon smiles down by night.

The Water still misses his friends. Twice a day he creeps up the shore to see if they have come home.

Then, very politely, he goes away again.
