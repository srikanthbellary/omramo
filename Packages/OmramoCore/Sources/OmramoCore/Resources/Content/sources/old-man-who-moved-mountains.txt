# Part I

# --- page 1
Long ago in China, an old man lived facing two great mountains.

His name was Yu Gong, which means Foolish Old Man. He was nearly ninety.

The mountains were called Taihang and Wangwu. They stood right in front of his door, as high as the clouds.

# --- page 2
To reach the market, Yu Gong had to walk all the way round them. It took days and days.

One evening he came home with sore feet and a dusty beard. He called his whole family together.

His wife, his sons and his grandchildren all sat down.

“Those mountains are in our way,” he said. “Let us dig them away.”

# --- page 3
His sons nodded. His grandchildren cheered.

Yu Gong’s wife folded her arms. “Husband, you puff going up our little hill,” she said. “And where will you put all that earth and stone?”

Everybody looked east, where the river ran away to the sea.

“In the sea,” said Yu Gong. “The sea has plenty of room.”

# --- page 4
The next morning they began. Crack! The sons broke the rock. The grandchildren dug the earth.

Yu Gong filled the baskets, and his wife tied them tight.

Each carrying pole had a basket at both ends. Up it went, onto a shoulder.

Dig, lift, carry, tip. Dig, lift, carry, tip.

# --- page 5
Next door lived a little boy who was just losing his milk teeth.

He watched over the wall. Then he came skipping across the yard.

“I can carry too!” he said, with a gap in his smile.

“Then here is a basket your size,” said Yu Gong.

# --- page 6
It was a long, long way to the sea. They set off when snow lay on the path.

Splash! In went the earth and stones. Then they turned for home, and the peaches were ripe when they got there.

And the mountains? They looked just as big as before.

Still the family kept on, basket by basket.

# --- page 7
The path to the sea passed a bend in the river. There lived Zhi Sou, whose name means Wise Old Man.

Every day he watched the baskets go by. One day he laughed out loud.

“Yu Gong, you are old and tired!” he called. “You could not pull one blade of grass off those mountains!”

# Part II

# --- page 8
Yu Gong set down his baskets and looked at Zhi Sou.

He did not shout. He did not sulk. He rubbed his sore shoulder and smiled.

“You are right, I am old,” he said. “One day I will be gone. But I have children.”

# --- page 9
“My children will have children,” said Yu Gong. “Their children will have children too, and on and on.”

He pointed up at the two grey peaks.

“My family grows. The mountains do not. So why should we not finish?”

Zhi Sou opened his mouth. He shut it again. The Wise Old Man had no answer at all.

# --- page 10
So the work went on. Dig, lift, carry, tip.

Winter came, and summer came. The little boy grew big new teeth.

Some days the family sang. Some days they grumbled. Every day they dug.

But high above the top of a mountain, somebody was listening.

# --- page 11
It was the Lord of the Sky. He had heard every scrape of the hoes and every word by the river.

He had never known hearts so steady. That family would never stop.

That night he sent two giants, the strongest there ever were.

One took Taihang on his back. One took Wangwu. Away they tiptoed while everybody slept.

# --- page 12
In the morning Yu Gong picked up his hoe and opened the door. He stopped. He rubbed his eyes.

No Taihang. No Wangwu. Only sky, and a wide plain, golden in the sun.

“Yu Gong!” shouted the little boy, hopping at the gate. “Who tipped them all?”

# --- page 13
Now a road ran straight to the south, flat as a table. The family walked to market and were home before dark.

Zhi Sou came up from the river bend to stare. He still had no answer.

Yu Gong’s wife went to hang up the baskets.

“Keep them handy,” said Yu Gong. “I have been looking at our little hill.”
