# Part I

# --- page 1
An old man and his wife lived below the green hills.

Their house had a thick straw roof. Behind it a bamboo grove whispered in the wind.

The old man had a small friend, a brown Sparrow. Every morning she hopped along the porch to meet him.

“Chun, chun!” she sang. “Good morning!”

# --- page 2
Every evening the old man came home from the hills with firewood on his back.

He sat down with his bowl of rice. He took one bite. Then he put a few grains in his hand.

“One for me,” he said, “and one for you, little friend.”

The Sparrow ate from his hand, grain by grain by grain.

# --- page 3
One day the old man went up the hill to cut wood. It was washing day.

His wife, the old woman, had a heap of clothes to wash. Her back ached. Her hands were sore.

She had cooked a bowl of rice paste to make the clothes smooth and stiff. But when she reached for it, the bowl was empty.

# --- page 4
Down hopped the Sparrow and bowed her little head.

“I ate it,” she said. “I thought it was my breakfast. I am sorry.”

But the old woman was too tired and too cross to listen. Snip! went her scissors, and nipped the tip of the Sparrow’s tongue.

“Shoo!” she cried. “Be off!” And the Sparrow flew away over the bamboo.

# --- page 5
That evening the porch was quiet. No hop, no song, no small brown friend.

“Where is my Sparrow?” asked the old man.

“She ate my paste, so I chased her away,” said the old woman, banging the pots.

The old man could not eat his rice. At first light he tied on his sandals and went to find her.

# --- page 6
The bamboo stood tall and green on every side. A clear, cold stream ran over the stones.

A white heron stood in the water, as still as a post.

“Sparrow, Sparrow, where is your home?” called the old man.

The heron only blinked. The bamboo only creaked.

# --- page 7
He walked on, up the hill and down the hill. “Sparrow, Sparrow, where is your home?”

The sun climbed high. His legs grew tired. “Sparrow, Sparrow, where is your home?”

“Chun, chun! Here I am!”

There on a little gate sat the Sparrow. “My tongue is quite well again,” she said. “Come in, dear friend!”

# --- page 8
Behind the gate stood a small house of pale wood.

All the Sparrow’s family came and bowed. The old man bowed back.

“You are our guest,” said the Sparrow. “Sit here, in the best place.”

They brought him hot tea and rice cakes and sweet red beans. Then the young sparrows danced, flip, flap, flutter, and the old man laughed.

# Part II

# --- page 9
When the sun dipped low, it was time to go home.

The sparrows carried in two baskets. One was big and heavy. One was small and light.

“A gift for you,” said the Sparrow. “Please choose.”

“I am old,” said the old man, “and the small one is enough. Thank you, little friend.”

# --- page 10
At home the old man lifted the lid of the small basket.

Out shone gold coins, rolls of silk, and coral as red as berries.

“There was a big one too?” cried the old woman. “And you left it behind? I shall fetch it myself!”

“We have enough,” said the old man. But she was already tying on her sandals.

# --- page 11
The old woman marched through the bamboo. Stamp, stamp, stamp.

She did not bow. She did not say good day. She did not say sorry.

“No tea for me,” she said. “I have come for the big basket.”

“Then please take it,” said the Sparrow, and she bowed as politely as ever.

# --- page 12
The big basket was heavy. It bumped on her back all along the hill road.

“Gold,” she puffed. “Silk. Coral. Heaps and heaps of it!”

The evening sky turned pale and pink. Home was still far away, and she could not wait one step more.

She sat down on the road and pulled off the lid.

# --- page 13
Buzz! Out flew a cloud of bees.

Plop! Out hopped a fat brown toad, and sat on her foot.

Under the toad there was only mud and stones. Not one coin. Not one thread of silk.

“Oh, oh, oh!” cried the old woman. She ran all the way home, with the bees humming behind her.

# --- page 14
The old woman tumbled in with mud on her nose.

“I was cross and greedy,” she puffed, “and I am sorry. The small basket was enough.”

After that, she put out rice each morning for every sparrow in the hills. The old man’s friend always came first.

And on washing days, she made two bowls of paste. Just in case.
