# Part I

# --- page 1
This story walked from India to Persia with the caravans.

It rode on swaying camels, over mountains and across wide sands.

At every well where the camels drank, somebody told it. Beside every fire, somebody told it again.

Now it has come all the way to you.

# --- page 2
In the dry golden hills of Persia lay a green valley.

A cold stream ran through it. Deer drank there, and gazelles, and a fox, and a slow old tortoise, and one small hare.

It would have been the best valley in the world, but for the Lion.

# --- page 3
Every day the Lion stood on his rock and roared.

Every day he chased the deer from the grass and the gazelles from the stream. He chased the fox. He even chased the tortoise, which was not hard.

Nobody could eat in peace. Nobody could drink in peace. Nobody could even nap.

# --- page 4
One evening the animals met under the great plane tree.

“I have not finished a meal since spring,” said the Tortoise.

“We cannot fight him,” said the Gazelle. “We cannot outroar him. But perhaps we can make a bargain with him.”

In the long grass sat the little Hare. He said nothing. He only listened.

# --- page 5
So they all went to the foot of the Lion’s rock.

“Great Lion,” said the Gazelle, “stay in your den. Every day, one of us will bring your dinner to you.”

The Lion yawned. He liked chasing. But he liked resting more.

“Agreed,” he said. “But it must come every day. And it must never be late.”

# --- page 6
So it began. One day it was fish from the stream. The next day it was honey from the rocks. The Tortoise brought figs, and that took him from dawn to dark.

The valley was quiet now. But was the Lion pleased? He was not.

“More!” he grumbled from his den. “Bigger! Sooner!”

And each day he said it a little louder.

# --- page 7
Then came the day it was the little Hare’s turn.

But the Hare did not set off. He sat under the plane tree and twitched his long ears.

“Hurry, Hare!” said the Gazelle. “The sun is climbing. He will be so angry!”

“Good,” said the Hare. “I have a plan, and it needs an angry lion. Give me a little time.”

# Part II

# --- page 8
At noon the Hare hopped up to the rock, with empty paws.

The Lion had been waiting since breakfast. His tummy growled. Then he growled.

“Late!” he roared. “Late, and nothing in your paws! Where is my dinner?”

The Hare bowed so low that his ears touched the dust.

# --- page 9
“O King,” he said, “I was bringing you a fine fat fish. But another lion stopped me on the road.”

“Another lion?” said the Lion.

“A big one,” said the Hare. “He said he was king of this valley. Then he took your dinner and ate it.”

The Lion’s tail went stiff. “King? In my valley? Show me!”

# --- page 10
So the Hare led the way, and the Lion stamped along behind.

Up the hot hills they went, past grey rocks and tall dark cypress trees. The dust puffed. The sun beat down.

“Is it far?” puffed the Lion.

“Not far, O King,” said the Hare. And he hopped a little faster.

# --- page 11
At last they came to an old, deep well, with a ring of stones around it.

The Hare stopped. He began to shake, from his ears to his tail.

“He lives down there,” he whispered. “I am too afraid to look alone. Hold me, O King, and we will look together.”

So the Lion picked him up in his paws.

# --- page 12
The Lion leaned over the stones and looked down.

Far below, in the dark round water, a lion looked up. And in his paws he held a small hare.

“So it is true!” roared the Lion. “Get out of my valley!”

And from deep in the well, a roar came back: my valley … valley … valley.

Nobody roars back at a lion.

# --- page 13
The Lion dropped the Hare, and he leaped.

Splash!

The water was cold. The water was deep. And there was no other lion in it at all.

He scrambled up the rough stones, paw over paw, and flopped out onto the dust. His mane hung flat. He looked no bigger than a wet cat.

# --- page 14
The Lion did not roar. He crept away over the hills, dripping, and he never came back.

The Hare skipped home to the green valley.

That evening the deer ate, the gazelles drank, and the Tortoise finished a whole meal, very slowly.

And if you ever look down a well and a face looks up at you — wave. Do not roar.
