# Omramo retelling, Low tier. Manuscript for the text-fidelity check.

# Part I

# --- page 1
High in a tree on a river bank lived a clever Monkey.

All day he ate sweet fruit and watched the wide river go by.

In that river lived a hungry Mother Crocodile and her son.

“My son,” she said one day, “I want a Monkey’s heart for my supper. Go and fetch me one.”

# --- page 2
The little Crocodile blinked. “How can I catch a Monkey? I cannot climb, and he never swims.”

“Use your head,” said his mother. “You will find a way.”

So the little Crocodile floated in the warm water. He thought, and he thought, and he thought.

# --- page 3
At last he had an idea. He swam to the tall tree.

“Friend Monkey!” he called. “There is ripe fruit on the island, the sweetest you ever tasted. Come and see!”

The Monkey looked across the water. The island was far away.

“I would love to,” he said, “but I cannot swim.”

# --- page 4
“I can,” said the Crocodile. “Sit on my back and I will carry you.”

The Monkey did love ripe fruit. Down he jumped, onto the Crocodile’s bumpy back.

“Off we go!” said the Crocodile, and he pushed out into the river.

“What a fine ride!” laughed the Monkey.

# --- page 5
But in the middle of the river, the Crocodile began to sink. Lower and lower he went.

Water splashed over the Monkey’s toes, then his knees, then his tummy.

“Stop, stop!” he cried. “Why are you going under? I told you, I cannot swim!”

# --- page 6
“I am sorry, Monkey,” said the Crocodile. “My mother wants your heart for her supper. I am taking you to her.”

The Monkey was very frightened. But he did not scream, and he did not fight. He sat still and thought fast.

# --- page 7
“My heart?” he said. “What a pity! I never take it on rides. I keep it safe at home, in my tree.”

“Then we must go back and get it,” said the Crocodile.

So he turned around and swam all the way back to the river bank.

# --- page 8
Whisk! The moment they touched the bank, the Monkey shot up his tree, right to the very top.

“My heart is up here!” he called down. “It was with me all the time. Come and get it, if you can!”

The Crocodile could only snap his teeth and swim home.

# Part II

# --- page 9
After that day, the Monkey moved to a new tree, far down the river.

From there he could reach an island full of fruit trees. A big rock stood halfway across, like a stepping stone.

Hop to the rock, hop to the island. Easy!

But the Crocodile found him, and watched.

# --- page 10
One day the Monkey feasted on the island until the sun went down.

While he ate, the Crocodile climbed onto the rock. He lay very flat and very still.

“When he jumps on me in the dark,” he thought, “I will catch him.”

# --- page 11
At dusk the Monkey came to the water’s edge. He stopped.

“That is odd,” he thought. “My rock looks taller tonight.”

So he tried a trick. “Hello, Rock!” he called. The rock said nothing.

“Rock, my friend, why do you not answer me tonight?”

# --- page 12
“Oh dear,” thought the Crocodile. “The rock must talk to him every night. I had better answer.”

“Yes, Monkey! What is it?” he called.

The Monkey laughed. “So it is you, Crocodile!”

“It is,” said the Crocodile. “And this time I will catch you.”

# --- page 13
“Then you have won,” said the Monkey. “Open your mouth wide, and I will jump right in.”

Now, the Monkey knew a secret. When a crocodile opens its mouth very wide, its eyes squeeze shut.

The Crocodile opened his mouth. His eyes closed tight.

# --- page 14
The Monkey jumped. But not into the mouth! He landed on the Crocodile’s head, bounced to the bank, and ran up his tree.

“Monkey,” called the Crocodile, “you are too clever for me. I will leave you alone.”

“Thank you,” said the Monkey. “But I will keep one eye open, all the same.”
