# Part I

# --- page 1
Long ago, in the misty blue mountains, Possum had a splendid tail.

It was long and thick and bushy, and it swept the ground behind him.

Every morning he sat in the sun and combed it until it shone.

And every morning he sang, “Look at my tail! Oh, look at my tail!”

# --- page 2
Rabbit lived close by. Rabbit had a tail too, but it was nothing much. It was only a bit of fluff.

“Good morning, Rabbit!” Possum would call. “Have you seen my tail today?”

“I saw it yesterday,” said Rabbit. “And the day before. And the day before that.”

But Possum was already singing again.

# --- page 3
Now, Rabbit was the one who carried the news. One morning he came hopping up the path.

“Possum! There is to be a great dance tonight. All the animals are coming. Will you be there?”

Possum stroked his tail. “I will come,” he said, “if I may have the best seat. Everyone should be able to see my tail.”

# --- page 4
Rabbit’s ears twitched. He was tired of that tail. He was tired of it right down to his toes.

Then a trick popped into his head, and he smiled.

“The best seat is yours,” he said. “And I will send someone to comb your tail and dress it for the dance.”

“How kind!” said Possum.

# --- page 5
That afternoon, Cricket came to Possum’s den.

Cricket is so good at cutting hair that the Cherokee often call him the barber.

“Rabbit sent me,” he chirped. “Lie down, shut your eyes, and leave it all to me.”

So Possum stretched out in the warm sun and shut his eyes.

# --- page 6
Cricket combed the tail from top to tip. Then he took a long red string.

“I will wrap it round and round,” he said, “to keep every hair smooth until the dance.”

Round and round went the red string. And close behind the string, very softly, went Cricket.

Snip! Snip, snip.

# --- page 7
“What is that tickling?” asked Possum, with his eyes still shut.

“Only my comb,” said Cricket. Snip, snip. Soon the tail was wrapped from top to tip, as neat as a red ribbon.

“There,” said Cricket. “Do not untie it until you dance.”

“I shall be the finest sight in the woods,” said Possum.

# Part II

# --- page 8
That evening, all the animals came to the clearing.

Bear came, and Deer, and slow old Turtle. The Raccoons came, and a whole row of Squirrels.

Possum had the best seat, just as Rabbit had promised. His tail lay beside him in its red string.

“Wait until they see,” he whispered.

# --- page 9
At last it was Possum’s turn to dance. He stepped into the middle of the circle and pulled the red string loose.

He did not look down. He looked at all the faces around him, and he sang.

“Look at my tail! Oh, look at my tail!”

The animals gave a great shout.

# --- page 10
Possum was pleased. He danced around the circle and sang louder. “Look how long it is! Look how it sweeps the ground!”

The animals shouted again. Somebody giggled.

“Look how thick it is! Look how soft!” sang Possum.

Now everyone was laughing. Bear laughed until he had to sit down.

# --- page 11
Possum stopped. “Why are they laughing?” he wondered.

He looked at Rabbit. Rabbit was looking at the sky.

So, at last, Possum looked down.

There was his tail. There was not one hair on it. It was as thin and bare as a lizard’s tail.

# --- page 12
Possum opened his mouth to speak. Nothing came out.

He opened it to sing. Nothing came out.

His ears went hot. He was so surprised, and so ashamed, that he could not say one word.

So he did the only thing he could think of. Flop! He rolled over on his back, and he grinned.

# --- page 13
The laughing soon stopped, and the dance went on. But Possum’s tail has been bare from that day to this.

He never sang about it again.

And when a possum is taken by surprise, he still rolls over on his back and grins.

He is not saying a word. He is only waiting for everyone to stop looking.
